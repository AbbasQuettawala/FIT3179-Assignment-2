Data sources and preparation notes
One Country, Different Feeds

These notes retain the detailed source and preparation information behind the
short credits on the website. Source checks: 6 October 2026.

1. ACMA: How we watch and listen to content (Excel) (https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/How%20we%20watch%20and%20listen%20to%20content%20data%20tables.xlsx). Survey year: 2025. Report released 5 March 2026; the file’s web address is dated February. Earlier surveys provide the 2017–2025 trends. The questions cover weekly viewing (QF4), average hours for each format (QF5), video services used in six months (QF7), devices used at home (QF1), weekly listening (QH3) and listening locations (QH16). Workbook proportions are multiplied by 100 to show percentages. Participant counts come from “Column n”, not the number of people who answered yes. Age and city/regional groups come from the PBI BANNER sheets. Rankings use the same eight services each year. The horizon chart compares each activity with its first measured year. Free-to-air TV included self-recorded shows until 2021. People could choose several answers; small differences may reflect who was surveyed. See ACMA’s copyright policy (https://www.acma.gov.au/copyright) and any notices on the source files.

2. ACMA: How we use the internet (Excel) (https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/How%20we%20use%20the%20internet%20data%20tables.xlsx). Survey year: 2025; published February 2026. Question QD8 asks about online news and information in the past six months. The chart keeps the survey’s rules about who was asked and uses the participant count for each age group. Percentages are weighted, rather than calculated directly from participant counts. Using online information does not necessarily mean reading news daily or trusting it. See ACMA’s copyright policy (https://www.acma.gov.au/copyright).

3. RMIT / Swinburne / Telstra: Australian Digital Inclusion Index 2025 (PDF) (https://digitalinclusionindex.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ADII-Report-2025_V6-Remediated.pdf). Data collected in 2024. State scores and the capital-city/regional comparison are on page 11; the method is on page 10. Scores run from 0 to 100. They are not percentages and do not measure media use. State scores are copied from the source, not averaged to make a new national score. The way affordability is measured changed in 2025, so this page does not compare scores with earlier editions. See the official dashboard and its CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence terms (https://dashboard.digitalinclusionindex.org.au/Total.aspx).

4. University of Canberra: Digital News Report: Australia 2026 (https://www.canberra.edu.au/research/centres/nmrc/digital-news-report-australia). The three trust figures describe news people use, news on social media and news from AI chatbots. They come from this report’s survey of Australian news consumers, not the ACMA participants in the age chart. They are shown separately to avoid mixing the two surveys. See the report for permission to reuse its findings.

5. Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS): ASGS 2026 state and territory boundaries (https://geo.abs.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/ASGS2026/STE/MapServer). Released 22 July 2026. Maps include the eight states and territories, but not the ABS category “Other Territories”. The outlines are simplified at 0.05 degrees. Mapshaper 0.6.113 (https://mapshaper.org/) repairs the outlines around NSW and the ACT, removes small errors, keeps 30% of the outline points and saves them as TopoJSON map files. The geographic maps use Australian Albers equal-area projection, which keeps land areas in proportion. Labels are placed for readability; the ACT label has a line pointing to its location. Population circles are moved to avoid overlap. These simplified maps are for display, not precise measurements of individual sites. See ABS copyright and credit requirements (https://www.abs.gov.au/website-privacy-copyright-and-disclaimer).

6. ACMA: licensed broadcast transmitter register (https://www.acma.gov.au/list-transmitters-licence-broadcast) (download the Excel files as a ZIP (https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/BroadcastTransmitterExcel.zip)). Data dated 13 July 2026. Only records marked “Issued” in the AM, FM and DR sheets are used, within the eight states and territories. Temporary licences are excluded. Duplicate day/night entries are removed using the band, licence, technical specification and site ID; records are then grouped by site ID. Coordinates in degrees, minutes and seconds (GDA94) are converted to decimal degrees, including entries rounded to 60 seconds. Small differences between coordinate systems are not visible at this map scale. No signal coverage is calculated, and a licence does not prove a transmitter is operating. Adapted from ACMA under CC BY 2.5 AU (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/au/).

7. ABS: National, state and territory population, March 2026 (https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/national-state-and-territory-population/mar-2026). Released 17 September 2026. Figures come directly from the March 2026 population entries in the Data1 sheet of Table 4 (Excel) (https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/national-state-and-territory-population/mar-2026/310104.xlsx). These estimates may be revised. Circle size shows March 2026 population; colour shows digital inclusion from 2024, so the two measures describe different years. Population includes all ages in the eight states and territories, excluding “Other Territories”. Circle areas, not widths, grow in proportion to population; circles are moved apart so they do not overlap. Population is not multiplied by the inclusion score to count people left out, and it is not a count of media users. See ABS CC BY 4.0 licence terms (https://www.abs.gov.au/website-privacy-copyright-and-disclaimer).
